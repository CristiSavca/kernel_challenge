# V202 mixed prefill and verification headroom

The archived eight-H200 V41 server trace supplies **228 potential shared target calls** across seven repetitions. The four-user and 16-user schedules contain 70 and 99 opportunities. The 32-user schedule contains 59, and the one-user schedule has none. At 16 users, granting every opportunity a hypothetical 50 ms saving on the repetition’s wall clock gives **1.031×** raw throughput. The physical wall time includes queueing and all client-visible phases. Actual costs on V198 or V202 have yet to be measured. ## Mixed head and draft context

The replay holds V41’s real prompt sizes and contemporaneous running groups fixed, reads the captured prefill and verify graph grids, and applies the pinned whole-cohort bucket rule. This yields 0/66/99/55 opportunities at 1/4/16/32 users. V194's uniquely identifiable continuing full chunks add 0/4/0/4 local possibilities. Other full chunks lack reliable continuation identity. Each counted continuation retains the original final graph bucket by moving exactly eight prompt rows per tail request to a later chunk. These calculations do not establish that every opportunity can co-exist after a live scheduler changes request progress. [V160 admission](../v160_mixed_dflash_serving/scheduler_admission.md) · [V194 reconstruction](../v194_mixed_decode_chunk_budget/trace.json)

For P prompt requests covering Q prompt tokens and T decode requests, the mixed target body handles Q+8T rows. The checkpoint LM head scores the last token of each prompt and all eight positions of every tail, for **P+8T complete FP32 vocabulary rows**, each 202,048 elements wide. In a selected-row SP gather, each of eight ranks supplies P+8T BF16 hidden rows, and the destination gathers 8(P+8T) before indexing the true owner. With a larger selection, gathering the whole sharded hidden tensor can be cheaper. The table’s endpoint counts apply the context minimum of 128 local rows per rank and the strict criterion P+8T below the number of rows in its local shard. Substituting the captured bucket’s shard for the live TP-rounded shard yields the same counts in this trace. Gathering 8,192 BF16 context channels after this operation replaces gathering 40,960 raw tap channels. Under the live TP-rounded geometry, eligible context-gather destination bytes fall from **9.17 to 1.83 GiB**, **6.64 to 1.33 GiB**, and **12.12 to 2.42 GiB** over the 4/16/32-user traces. Pure prompt V198 already has the owner FC and a paired KV option. ## Latency and the four-load objective

The V41 client supplied individual timestamps for 767 requests. Replicating V195’s reconstruction reproduces all seven end-to-end repetition walls within 6 ms and rung rates within 0.06 tokens/s/GPU. Cold TTFT, ensuing-turn TTFT and post-first-token streaming cover different visible intervals. Users wait together, so their summed time shares and the measured last-user wall are distinct.

For each whole-phase sensitivity, all other user intervals and gaps stay fixed and the last-finishing user is recomputed per repetition. The mixed scenarios separately grant a fixed net reduction to each repetition’s makespan for every candidate call, then recompute its raw throughput with all original tokens. They give every credit direct critical-path impact, omit changes to future admissions and assume that the mixed head and context costs are already included in that credit. A live comparison must measure the net value and whether it appears in TTFT, streaming, or both. Four real commitments per tail appearance would account for 0/3.23/11.59/5.40% of generated tokens respectively. A committed token still needs verification and does not correspond to an equal fraction of runtime. V64’s scored four-load frontier remains **1.4119×**. A 4× score needs a further 2.833× at fixed interactivity and at least one raw point above roughly 39,984 tokens/s/GPU on the published stock frontier. These shares make the largest unaddressed phases explicit: the one-user path receives no mixed-call benefit, and most high-concurrency cold TTFT remains outside an identified mixed continuation. A V202 timing should report all four raw throughput and per-user rates, first/later TTFT, realized admission counts, and head/context branch counts alongside the V198 control.
