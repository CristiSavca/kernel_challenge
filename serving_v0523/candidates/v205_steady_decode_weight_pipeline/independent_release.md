# Grouped decode W2 stage lifetime

I inspected the emitted parent and candidate SM90a disassemblies for all three native M widths, 8, 16 and 24. Each image issued the same 32 FP32-accumulating BF16 HGMMA instructions, alternating two output register bands. The parent released each shared stage after its sixth instruction and drained the remaining pair separately. V205 held each stage through its eighth instruction, with a zero-outstanding-group drain before its stage release. For each candidate the four releases followed HGMMA numbers 8, 16, 24 and 32. The final epilogue signalled a separate barrier. The first register word of each general-register destination in the interval was checked against all four words of every still-live register-sourced HGMMA fragment. The candidate compiler receipt separately records vector-register write checks, zero spills and 96 registers in every candidate and parent.

The pinned loop starts three stages before waiting on a consumer release. A producer that has received all 512 consumer arrivals on stage i may publish stage i+3. At the consumer's next-stage wait inside tile i, that consumer has already retired tile i−1, and tile i+1 was made available by the release of tile i−2. Four warpgroups can progress independently without permitting an operand tile to be overwritten. An exhaustive state exploration found 11,640 reachable states and 19,775 transitions over 35 slice counts from 1 through 128, including counts below the pipeline depth. Every reachable terminal state completed all workers and producers. The end-of-job path skips the late prefetch when there is no next tile. The subsequent last register transform has no output consumer before the next job reinitializes its operands.

The no-argument fixture verifies retained image hashes and measurements and replays the complete state model. The additional raw-byte verifier accepts a reconstructed compiler stage. The original compiler scratch was retired after the first independent SASS inspection and before that extended verifier ran. The binary review covers generated order and resource counts. Timing and progress on H200 still require a physical run.
