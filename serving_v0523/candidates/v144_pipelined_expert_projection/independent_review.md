# V144 input and packed-weight pipeline

The frozen W13 loop reads one K slice ahead and W2 reads two. Both adapters require positive input widths divisible by 128, so even the smallest supported input contains eight 16-channel slices. The prologue fetches indices below the lookahead. Each steady iteration fetches the next legal slice, consumes the oldest register window, then shifts it. The epilogue consumes the final one or two entries in order. Across 256 width/mode combinations, all 132,096 slices were fetched and consumed exactly once in ascending K order. The independent audit checked 1,769,472 input, packed bank and scale bounds, including the final warp lane, gate/up plane and tile of the last expert. Padded route slots initialize their activation fragment to zero and avoid reading an input row. [Schedule and addressing fixture](independent_schedule.py) · [Results](independent_schedule.json)

The archived SM90a disassembly has an identifiable steady-loop back edge for each projection. W13 retains eight BF16 tensor-core instructions and two 64-bit plus two 128-bit packed loads per iteration across 327 instruction sites. W2 retains four tensor-core instructions, one 64-bit load and one 128-bit load across 189 sites. Neither steady loop contains a local-memory load or store. The parent's loops have 328 and 170 sites respectively. Scheduling the next fragment earlier can hide load latency; additional instructions and register pressure can offset it.
