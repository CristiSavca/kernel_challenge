# FP8 source lifetime and allocation checks

The 256-row FA3 guard can overwrite private verification-tail rows after the native writer scatters those rows into the physical cache. The pinned CUDA kernel fetches both source values into registers before enabling a dependent launch. It resolves the launch stream through the calling TVM FFI device stream. The two-stream fallback makes the V scatter wait for the current stream, then makes the current stream wait for that scatter before returning. [Source paths and SHA-256 digests](independent_lifetime.json)

A delayed CPU replay ran the actual allocation, writer dispatch and host pack on noncontiguous BF16 inputs spanning all 65,536 encodings across K and V. Nine cases covered zero and nonzero prefixes, nonsequential physical slots, tails shorter and longer than 256, and distinct K/V contents. All 10,953,216 physical cache bytes agreed with independent converted tensors. All 9,880,320 FP8 input bytes matched. Prefix, current and guard rows were exact, and the remainder of a 320-row private tail retained its published values. Removing the return-stream join corrupted the V cache and its observed tail in the negative control. The native CUDA store and Triton refill use delayed host copy probes in this fixture. [Replay and counterexample](independent_lifetime.json)

Twenty-one one-row-below, exact-limit and one-row-above cases covered seven geometries. Each admitted arena plus the targeted FP32 verifier workspace fit 32 MiB. The arena covers the larger of the head with its guard and all current writer rows. The subsequent tail invocation charges the arena's full byte count. The parent planner retains its earlier packed-plus-writer bound and can reject a shape before attempting aliasing. Native FA3 temporaries, allocator reservation and whole-server peak require hardware measurement. [Thresholds](independent_lifetime.json)
