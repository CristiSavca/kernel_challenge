# V112 exact FP8 and expert tile reuse on H200

The FP8 decomposition considered here fails the full-K accumulation test and has no compute-throughput advantage at equal tensor-core efficiency. The alternative cluster schedule offers a narrower, measurable opportunity. It would need long-prefill Humming counters that show repeated activations missing L2 before spending a check. V112 is retired as a production candidate. Its deliverable is the reproducible analysis below. The measured V64 and graph-managed V102 remain the reference implementations. ## Exact operand test

The CPU fixture enumerates all 16 signed E2M1 codes and all 127 legal positive-or-zero E4M3 scale bytes. It applies V64's exact subnormal-to-zero scale preparation. Each resulting product fits BF16. Only 1,502 of 2,032 code/scale pairs fit one unscaled E4M3. Another exponent scale could help range, but 444 pairs require five or six significand bits. E5M2 accepts 936 pairs directly and 1,096 pairs exceed its three-bit significand. These counts describe encodings and make no assumption about checkpoint frequencies. In a normal BF16 binade, eight of the 128 significands fit one E4M3 at its native scale. The fixture also checks the six-significant-bit product 1.5 × 1.375 = 2.0625. [Fixture](crossover.py) · [Results](crossover.json)

Splitting both operands into two exact terms gives four ordinary FP8 dot products. Splitting only activations and arranging for exact single-FP8 weights still needs two. The H200 SXM dense peaks used here are 989.5 BF16 and 1,979 FP8 TFLOP/s, after removing the sparsity multiplier from the published figures. At two products, FP8 needs to sustain a higher fraction of peak than BF16 to pay for conversion. At four products it needs over twice the BF16 fraction before conversion and extra accumulations. The checkpoint's group-of-16 scale varies with output column and K group. Factoring it outside the dot would require separately scaled group partials. Hopper FP8 WGMMA consumes K32 and supplies operand sign controls. It does not apply a different E4M3 block scale to each output column and K16 half. [NVIDIA H200 specifications](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/h200/) · [NVIDIA PTX WGMMA reference](https://docs.nvidia.com/cuda/parallel-thread-execution/index.html#asynchronous-warpgroup-level-matrix-multiply-accumulate-instructions-wgmma-mma)

Separate exact dots can also change the BF16 output solely through FP32 accumulation. At K indices 0, 16 and 32 put activations 73.5, 2⁻⁹ and −73.5 and legal decoded weights 448, 1 and 448. All remaining activations are zero. A sequential full-K FP32 accumulator returns 0. Decomposing the two large activations into ±72 and ±1.5, accumulating the high and low dots independently in FP32, then adding yields 2⁻⁹. Both results survive rounding to BF16. This is an arithmetic counterexample to general order equivalence. Tensor-core reduction order and actual checkpoint frequency require hardware evidence. [Executable witness](crossover.py)

## Can clusters reduce repeated work?

The pinned Humming grouped SM90 kernels use 176×256×64 CTAs with 128,000 bytes of shared memory per CTA. Each compacted expert M tile covers 32 W13 output tiles and 16 W2 output tiles. The scheduler assigns N fastest for grouped GEMMs. Nearby CTAs can therefore share cached input even with independent TMA requests. A two-CTA N cluster can multicast one A tile to both. V69 applies this primitive to decode. Its dataflow is compatible with the grouped prefill schedule. [Humming 0.1.12 source](https://pypi.org/project/humming-kernels/0.1.12/) · [V63 layout check](../v63_humming_grouped_compact/README.md) · [V69 scheduler analysis](../v69_humming_decode_a_multicast/README.md)

For 16,384 top-eight tokens, 32 deterministic uniform draws over 256 experts produce 256 per-rank histograms. Each token routes to eight distinct experts. Each rank averages 3,306 W13 CTAs and 1,653 W2 CTAs. A perfect final wave yields 1.016× and 1.039× respectively at 132 resident CTAs. Removing all M padding also yields just 1.110× arithmetic relative to this schedule. Useful and padded work take 1.667 ms and 1.850 ms at peak BF16 rate. These floors exclude kernel launch, routing, dequantization, synchronization and activation costs. [Seeded geometry estimates](crossover.json)

| Per-rank volume at 16,384 tokens | W13 and W2 combined |

| Unique BF16 activation inputs | 256 MiB |
| Requested BF16 activations across N tiles, live rows | 6,144 MiB |
| Requested activations if all CTA row tails load | 6,819 MiB |
| Unique packed weights and scales | 864 MiB |
| Packed weights and scales across M tiles | 2,789 MiB |
| GEMM outputs written | 384 MiB |

For two-CTA A multicast, the executable model varies duplicate activation and weight HBM miss fractions independently from 0 to 1 and uses the greater of compute and memory floors. At 60% BF16 peak and 75% HBM peak, it gives 1.00× even when all repeated reads miss. At 90% BF16 peak with every repeated activation and weight read missing, it gives 1.32×. At 100% BF16 peak the same case gives 1.47× for the two GEMMs alone. The model assumes perfect multicast, zero additional synchronization and fixed efficiency. It excludes L2 bandwidth constraints and does not assign actual hit rates. [Full scenario table](crossover.json)

B multicast would share weights across M tiles. In the pinned vendor scheduler the grouped tile count uses a single-CTA M span at line 178 and then multiplies the selected M ordinal by the B cluster width at lines 216–217 of `scheduler.cuh`. Merely enabling the setting can select past the end of a grouped expert range. A custom scheduler would need per-expert cluster counts, matching peer barriers on incomplete groups, and masked work for unpaired tails. The two-way version could reduce logical B reads from 2,789 to 1,728 MiB on the fixture, while reserving 24% more CTA slots per real M tile. Cross-rank stripes trade local work for NVLink collectives and expert normalization dependencies. V103 evaluated the corresponding EP and expert-TP choices, including concentrated routing. [Vendor source](https://pypi.org/project/humming-kernels/0.1.12/) · [V103 topology analysis](../v103_hybrid_expert_tp_feasibility/README.md)

Pipelining W13 and W2 for different experts shares the same tensor cores. The aggregate minimum work remains 1.65 TFLOP/rank/layer. The opportunity is in exposed compute or communication gaps. Removing the W13 BF16 gate/up intermediate write and its activation read would save 512 MiB per rank before cache, equivalent to 0.112 ms at peak HBM bandwidth. Computing the same W13 output separately in several W2 output tiles would duplicate its dominant matrix product. The historical standalone H200 result was 5.83 ms for dense BF16 versus 6.62 ms for V40 at this size. The available hardware counter captures isolate M32 and M256 using a different standalone kernel, with 74.43% and 75.06% sustained DRAM throughput. Their measured reads were 1.009× and 1.007× their nominal packed request volume. These measurements cannot resolve the Humming long-prefill activation miss rate.
