# V175 verification collective feasibility

The archived H200 evidence favors keeping verification rows together through the expert bank. We examined overlapping the first half’s expert reduction and unpool projection with the second half's W13/W2 work. Splitting by tokens preserves each row's full-K calculation, EP ownership, original slot order and BF16 reduction boundary. It also makes both halves reread the bank of any expert they share. At the modeled 32-user case, the extra nominal weight requests are **833 MiB per rank per sparse layer** under uniform routing, or **481 MiB** for a random partition of a separate measured H200 routing histogram. Across all 25 archived passing standalone H200 MoE profiles, twice each job’s measured 128-row median exceeded its measured 256-row median for the baseline and every submitted implementation. V175 has no serving export or private reservation. [Reproduction and physical job identities](check.json)

## Token microbatch pipeline

The normal Muse path computes local weighted experts, reduces their BF16 result, then uses every reduced input feature in the unpooler. There is no same-row independence between these operations. Two groups of **different tokens** could use two streams after their shared router/pooler and join before the next layer. The second group’s routes must still be processed. With native eight-row packing at small loads, or W13/W2 packing up to 16/32 at medium loads, splitting raises the number of weight sweeps when the same expert occurs in both groups. The calculation covers 5,460 exhaustive small token partitions and the exact per-expert distributions at the production shapes. ## Partial projection groups

A separate exact approach forms two- or four-rank subgroups of the replicated verification batch. Each member uses a full-K output-column slice of the pool/unpool weights, then the subgroup gathers those columns in their original order. Compared with V142's full TP8 output projection, TP4 adds 16 MiB of cold weights per rank across both matrices. Its logical ring send saving is only 24, 96, 384 and 768 KiB per sparse layer at the four loads. TP2 triples both quantities. Ignoring launch startup and assuming 4.8 TB/s HBM, the respective break-even effective link bandwidths are 7.03, 28.13, 112.5 and 225 GB/s. Actual topology and latency determine whether a subgroup wins. This design adds no new way to reduce the useful matrix work below V142. It also needs matched subgroup communicators on every rank and must preserve the current residual/norm boundary and prompt dispatch. V119 covers token ownership across projections, V124 covers native multicast reduce-scatter for selected shapes, V148 shows why moving an input-feature partial across the unpool GEMM can change its rounded result, and V150 handles the long-prompt output projection. The recent V173 route-class split exposes a different opportunity: tiny and hot experts work on disjoint route slots without splitting a bank at a token boundary. Their two branches can finish independently until the weighted finalizer. A scheduler designed to overlap those existing branches, or a distributed trace demonstrating a large exposed same-layer collective, could change the decision. The official single-GPU layer task explicitly treats all-reduce and all-gather as identities. V175 used only archived results and host calculations.
