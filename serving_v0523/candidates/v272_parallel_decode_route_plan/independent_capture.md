All four plan buffers are allocated on the consuming main stream before the router stream waits. The side stream writes the plan from current routed IDs. The main stream rejoins after normalization and before the gathered expert GEMMs consume that plan. The final plan tuple keeps references through the expert call. The storage is 900, 3,204, 12,420 and 24,708 bytes per rank/layer at those four shapes. The earlier serial path allocated arrays of the same sizes later in the call. Earlier allocation can overlap other live tensors or affect graph-pool reuse. The comparison measures neither a new peak nor a safe reduction in reserved graph memory. Let the elapsed times of pooled projection, routing through top-k, normalization and plan construction be P, R, N and Q. The two-arm critical path changes from max(P,R)+N+Q to max(P+N,R+Q), assuming no resource contention. When routing finishes last on the original fork, the additional saving cannot exceed N. When pooling finishes last, it cannot exceed Q. At V68's 16-user check, four accepted tokens per pass divided by 48.0 tokens/s/user gives an approximate 83.33 ms period. Achieving the uniform 3× or 4× speed factors within that period would require 598 or 758 µs saved per eligible layer. These are demand thresholds. Neither this control nor the archived raw target verification records isolates N or Q in steady captured execution.
