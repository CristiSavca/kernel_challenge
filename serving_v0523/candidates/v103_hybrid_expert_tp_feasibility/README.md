# TP8 with sharded NVFP4 experts

**Decision. Keep V64 at EP8.** The measured parent cannot run its Humming experts at EP4, EP2 or EP1 through launch flags. A correct expert-TP implementation could reduce work on a busy rank, but it adds communication at every MoE layer. The available serving results do not establish the rank imbalance needed to pay for it. This directory contains a reproducible topology model. It contains no serving patch, hardware result or reservation. [Measured parent](../../runs/abd217baf0ed49fd.json) · [V64 source identity](../v64_humming_grouped_sequence_parallel/validation.json)

## What the pinned serving code supports

- With TP fixed at eight and MoE data parallelism off, EP4/2/1 implies expert TP2/4/8. The parallel runtime creates contiguous expert-TP groups and strided expert-EP groups. The Muse constructor rejects NVFP4 whenever expert TP exceeds one. Its separate GPTQ int4 path rejects mixed TP/EP and uses different weights and arithmetic. V64’s layernorm sequence-parallel admission independently requires TP4/EP4 or TP8/EP8. [Parallel groups](../../sglang/python/sglang/srt/distributed/parallel_state.py) · [Muse constructor](../../sglang/python/sglang/srt/models/muse_spark.py) · [V64 sequence-parallel changes](../v64_humming_grouped_sequence_parallel/patch.diff)
- Muse’s checkpoint copy narrows the leading expert dimension by EP rank and requires all remaining extents to match. Its NVFP4 constructor receives full hidden and intermediate dimensions. Humming repacks those local weights and scales. Each 4096-wide expert occupies 18 MiB for gate/up and 9 MiB for down, including per-group scale bytes. Thirty-two experts in each of 62 layers occupy 52.31 GiB per GPU, excluding small global scales and metadata. Keeping these shapes while halving EP would add another 52.31 GiB. V64 measured +1.9 GB/GPU against the judge’s +2 GB peak limit. [FP4 layer](../../sglang/python/sglang/srt/layers/muse_spark/muse_spark_fp4_moe.py) · [Measured peak](../../runs/abd217baf0ed49fd.json) · [Serving rules](../../RULES.md)
- W2 produces BF16 expert rows. Post-expert RMS spans the entire output width and precedes the weighted top-k sum. In the weight-first branch the norm weight is stored as BF16, while the input, multiplication, square and sum use FP32. The constructor’s final EP reduction assumes that a tensor-parallel expert has already reduced its output before RMS. Merely dividing K, rounding each partial to BF16 and summing changes valid results. With unit weights and BF16 inputs 256, 1, −256, 1, full-K FP32 accumulation rounds to 2, whereas separately rounded partials give 1. [Finalizer](../../sglang/python/sglang/kernels/ops/moe/muse_spark_post_expert_finalize_triton.py) · [Executable counterexample](traffic_model.py)

## A design that preserves full K

Within each group of T ranks, store disjoint gate/up **output** channels with their scales. Each rank computes its gate/up slice using the whole input K and preserves V64’s BF16 activation boundary. All-gather the resulting SiLU products within the group. Store W2 by **output** channel, so each owned output again uses the whole K. Recombine W2 channels for the original norm, or reconstruct its full FP32 moment reduction and channel-wise operations before the top-k combine. EP partials, original slot order, global scaling and V64’s output projection layout still need explicit handling.

At balanced top-8, a group sees approximately M×T valid routes for M input rows. Each rank receives M×(T−1)×4096×2 bytes of intermediate activations for one layer. At 16,384 rows this equals **128, 384 or 896 MiB per rank** for T=2, 4 or 8, before norm and output exchanges. At 128 rows the corresponding numbers are 1, 3 and 7 MiB. A chunked implementation can bound live scratch. A conventional W2-K split followed by a ring FP32 all-reduce of every routed full row costs four times this incoming activation traffic at equal dimensions. A reduce-scatter changes that cost and still requires an accumulation-order analysis. [Traffic calculations](traffic_model.json)

## How much work could be balanced?

Each logical expert sees the same routed rows at every T. Increasing T changes its output width per rank and the number of experts visible on that rank. Any savings must come from cross-rank load balance or better device scheduling. The model assumes perfect partitioning, one cold bank read per active expert and zero cost for collectives. It reports the ratio of mean busiest-rank loads at EP8 versus EP1 over identical draws. These are per-layer workload ratios.

| Global rows | Ideal cold-weight traffic ratio | Ideal routed arithmetic ratio |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 8 | 1.483× | 1.526× |
| 32 | 1.187× | 1.257× |
| 128 | 1.018× | 1.124× |
| 256 | 1.0003× | 1.088× |
| 16,384 | 1.000× | 1.011× |

The first simulation draws eight distinct uniform experts per row. An expert has probability 1−(31/32)^M of appearing at least once, or 98.28% and 99.97% at M=128 and 256. A second synthetic distribution drew four routes from 32 hot experts and four from the other 224. Placing all 32 hot experts together yielded 4.000× peak-to-mean routed work at M=16,384. Interleaving the exact same draws and experts across eight ranks yielded only 1.010×. The benefit therefore depends on which hot experts share an owner, how long that assignment remains hot and the share of end-to-end time in the affected layers. Per-expert popularity alone does not establish that benefit. [All simulated EP sizes, placements and seeds](traffic_model.json)

Our earlier standalone H200 diagnostic observed 39 local routes over 16 live experts for M=32 and 354 over 26 live experts at M=256, including one expert with 73 routes. Those are counts from a single owner and a different component workload. At M=32 its cold DRAM reads were 456,964,352 bytes for 452,984,832 unique weight bytes. The other owners’ counts, persistent all-layer imbalance and matched serving timing remain unmeasured. ## Can the existing expert load balancer move whole experts?

SGLang’s EPLB supports per-layer logical counts, fixed-size expert placement with zero redundancy and batched updates. Its default check interval is 1,000 steps. A supported model can also initialize a per-layer physical-to-logical map. The pinned Muse text model and its multimodal subclass lack both the model configuration hook and the per-layer packed weight registry. Automatic EPLB enables the statistics recorder, which asserts that the missing location metadata exists. A supplied explicit placement map returns no metadata for this model. The V64 weight loader slices contiguous experts and its local routing lookup initializes from the same contiguous range. [Model hooks](../../sglang/python/sglang/srt/eplb/expert_location.py) · [Recorder admission](../../sglang/python/sglang/srt/eplb/expert_distribution.py) · [EPLB manager](../../sglang/python/sglang/srt/eplb/eplb_manager.py) · [Omni model](../../sglang/python/sglang/srt/models/muse_spark_omni.py)

If an ordinary serving profile establishes persistent imbalance across owners, moving pairs of **intact** 27 MiB experts between ranks can preserve 32 unique banks per rank and avoid per-route tensor collectives. Such a port needs stable graph-visible maps for every fallback/dispatch path, all-rank publication after weight movement, preserved normalization and verified agreement after changing rank-wise top-k grouping. The generic updater allocates a temporary copy of one entire local layer, around 864 MiB for this bank. Reusing memory or moving bounded cycles would need a new peak measurement. A fixed remapping derived from the fixture has no evidentiary basis on the served workload.
