# V173 expert-list numerical replay

The production padded planner, sparse TMA consumer and multirow MMA consumer agreed with a dense reference across 33 routed projection pairs. The fixture checked 551,040 BF16 gate activations, 564,480 BF16 down outputs, 493,440 inactive cells and 101,376 input elements. Each geometric case ran under three asynchronous copy schedules. Six additional cancellation cases verified ascending full-K FP32 accumulation on both sparse and dense routes. The expected down value of 5.4424162954092026e−9 survived BF16 rounding. A mixed input contained expert lists of every length from zero through nine, as well as 15, 16, 17, 31, 32, 33 and 45. The test reused a stale previous plan, including valid positive slot IDs, then launched W13 and W2 in their ordinary phase order. It checked the contents of every untouched row after each producer and varied which projection qualified for TMA. Small seven- and eight-slot arenas exercised both sides of the capacity boundary; a separate sparse case used the 4,096-channel model width. During the thirty-nine runs, 51,669 stale tail indices remained beyond the planner’s required prefix, and 3,499,776 untouched intermediate or output elements stayed identical in the per-launch checks.

The fixture compiles the source bodies into host coroutines. It supplies CPU equivalents of warp ballots, matrix instructions, transfer completion and mbarriers. The emulated async engine executed 19,332 tensor copies and 7,344 activation-row copies. The results establish agreement on these inputs. Hopper timing, launch overhead and end-to-end throughput remain hardware questions.
