# Which expert rows can benefit

The scored V64 receipt reports concurrency and overall acceptance, throughput and latency. It contains no per-layer expert counts. The separate archived H200 MoE hardware counter run has one relevant observed rank-0 histogram at 256 model rows. Generated inputs and a different kernel produced those device counters. It provides a tested skew pattern for V173's geometry, without measuring V173's speed or the serving route distribution. | 1,024 slots, uniform distinct routing | 30.83 / 0.62 | 95.35% | 98.01% | 1.96× |

| 2,048 slots, standalone device diagnostic | 16 / 10 | 16.67% | 45.28% | 1.29× |

The first two rows use exact expectations when each token independently selects eight distinct experts uniformly from 256. The final column assumes equal cost per byte for both prior kernels, unchanged hot-expert cost and zero overhead. In the measured standalone histogram, only 59 of 354 local routes belong to tiny experts. Those 16 experts still account for 432 of the 954 MiB of nominal W13/W2 weight loads because even one route needs its whole bank. V173 keeps the existing weight-sweep counts and arithmetic. It can improve achieved throughput by staging and reusing those loads. Actual DRAM traffic, per-expert work time and the mix across ranks can differ substantially. [Model and reproduced receipt](independent_headroom.json)

**Parallelism and launch cost.** For each bank, both grids have 32 column bands × 32 expert IDs. The device-side row count sends 1–8 rows to the nine-warp producer/consumer kernel and more than eight to the native two-warp medium kernel. Empty experts exit in both. This adds two launches and 2,048 fast-exit CTAs per eligible sparse layer across both banks. Inherited SM90a register counts imply ceilings of roughly four nine-warp or eight two-warp resident CTAs per SM before other constraints. At 1,024 uniformly distributed slots, an average rank has about 986 useful tiny CTAs and 20 useful hot CTAs per bank. With 132 SMs those consume 1.87 and 0.019 CTA-capacity equivalents, respectively. The two serial launches and the hot rank’s tail still count even for a mostly tiny batch. At 2,048 uniform slots, the capacities are 1.15 and 0.40 with about 417 active hot CTAs. If added overhead is 5% of prior projection-pair time, tiny experts must improve by more than 5.4%, 9.2% and 12.4% respectively in the three rows above. Comparing capacity or bytes alone cannot establish elapsed time. The 16-user geometry is the stronger matched hardware test of the new kernel. A 32-user check additionally measures whether the serial hot-expert path offsets the benefit. The long first-turn prefill, low-slot workloads, other layers and DFLASH acceptance have their own costs. None of these modeled projection-pair ratios establishes a frontier gain.
