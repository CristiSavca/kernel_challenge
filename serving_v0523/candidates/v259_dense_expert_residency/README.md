# V259 retired the rotating dense expert bank

On V256, expanding a complete layer of NVFP4 experts to BF16 costs **3 GiB per GPU**. The recorded slot counts would release 6.116 GiB of private draft payload. Three extra FA graph tables and the write vector consume 48.002 MiB for the archived single-backend geometry, leaving only **70.571 MiB** after two dense layers. The 62-layer model needs 186 GiB per GPU to pin all such weights. Its grouped prompt and decode paths continue to share the packed checkpoint bank. At the beginning of a full-layer sweep at most two complete layers can be resident. Replacing least-recently-used layer banks achieves zero hits across four sweeps. Pinning arbitrary individual matrices is better. W13 occupies 64 MiB per local expert and W2 occupies 32 MiB. Pricing a conversion using V42's historical 42.2 and 24.4 µs per matrix respectively, the most valuable persistent set stores 194 W2 matrices and saves 4.734 ms across all 62 layers. Every weight has only one first use in the sweep. A newly introduced weight can help on a subsequent sweep. A weighted enumeration checked the first-use argument across all 24 orders and six capacities of a two-expert example, allowing arbitrary admission and eviction. At 75 and 150 grouped calls in the archived 16- and 32-user benchmark windows, the modeled savings relative to repeated V42 conversions are 0.355 and 0.710 seconds, or 0.215% and 0.233% of total wall time. V256 Humming dequantizes in its compute pipeline, so those saved microseconds do not transfer directly to V256. ## Captured capacity

V256's actual native target configurator still charges 9,600 bytes per full slot. At V68's measured 3,420,199 full slots, backing a persistent 3 GiB or 6 GiB workspace before final sizing yields 3,084,654 or 2,749,110 full slots when the byte budget binds. In the alternative scenario with 6 GiB of free slack above the token cap, all three workspace sizes preserve the original count. V41's measured 32-user one-copy envelope was 2,919,450 rows. Its request trace and V68's memory pool come from different deployments. Allocating a bank after final sizing requires measured peak headroom, stable workspace ownership and correct reuse across stream and graph replays. V68 measured 1.7 GB/GPU over stock against the challenge's 2 GB limit. V256 has no matched peak. ## Work a dense path must recover

At 16,384 prompt tokens, a single H200 measured 6.617 ms for V40 Marlin and 5.831 ms for V42's full dense path. An independently generated cuBLAS workload took 2.698 ms with 507 uniform rows per expert and all weights and rows prepared. V42's output differed from stock in 1.374% of the compared BF16 components at this size. All 2,032 finite nonnegative E4M3 scale/E2M1 encoding combinations after native subnormal flush yield an exactly representable BF16 product. Exact operand storage leaves the projection's accumulation order and separate BF16 global scaling, SiLU and route combination to verify. V44 introduced a 448-routes-per-expert dense crossover against Marlin. V197 now fuses the grouped-prompt W13 epilogue. V208 also trims tensor-core row tails and retains the same weight tile requests. These changes preclude a direct timing comparison to current Humming. A cold, full-layer conversion logically reads 864 MiB of packed codes and scales, writes 3,072 MiB of expanded weights and reads at least 3,072 MiB at their consumer. With a single consumer read, streaming those 6.844 GiB through HBM would take 1.531 ms at 4.8 TB/s. L2 reuse, repeated matrix loads and overlap determine actual HBM traffic and elapsed time. V112 and V208's uniform-routing model gives 2,789 MiB in packed requests before caching. A layer-major schedule that processes multiple independent chunks before replacing the bank could amortize the conversion. Even eliminating every measured V42 conversion would save only 9.910 or 19.820 seconds of the archived V41 windows under the stated cost model. That comparison already grants all 62 layers a free materialization. The model's 4× target would require 22.936 or 21.180 ms of net saving per eligible layer invocation at those loads, using 2.833× as the remaining uniform factor. The dense-bank hypothesis alone supplies no matching H200 evidence for that reduction.
