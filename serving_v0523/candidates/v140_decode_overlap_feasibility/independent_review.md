The ordinary V64 greedy path computes accept_lens, commit_lens and new_seq_lens on-device, calls on_publish(new_seq_lens), and then writes draft KV. The scheduler places the current forward ahead of processing the preceding batch’s result and orders schedule-stream writes with its existing shared-read event. Host arithmetic on an already-published CPU mirror does not introduce a new device read. The public receipt cannot establish any residual idle bubble.

Muse appends each operator-dtype target tap after its decoder layer. With the final tap at layer 59 of 62, starting the FC before layer 60 is feasible only through code inside target execution, including its captured graph. Hooking the worker after forward_batch_generation returns overlaps at most downstream head selection/acceptance. Draft-worker construction precedes the scheduler’s target graph capture, so a permanent, model-specific registration is possible in principle. A new side stream would need a fork and join in capture and replay. Original tap lifetimes, cross-rank collective ordering, hidden normalization and dynamically installed hooks still need to hold.

Let F be replicated FC latency, G the gather latency in an ideal output-sharded alternative, and H the portion a side stream actually hides, net of contention. If the local shard takes F/8, replicated overlap is faster only if H exceeds 7F/8 − G. The separate pooler measurement and division of mean commit count by median request generation rate supply a sensitivity study. They do not measure these FC or overlap intervals. Independent review agrees with retaining V140 as a feasibility result. A timed sharded projection and capture-level joined completion on an H200 would be needed to choose it over V120b.
