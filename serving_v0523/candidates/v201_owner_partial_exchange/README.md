# V201 sends prompt-row partial sums directly to their owners

V201 changes the two large reductions that end sequence-parallel attention and expert processing on V198. Each rank computes its ordinary BF16 partials, sends each peer just the rows it owns, and sums its own rows in ascending source-rank order with FP32 accumulation and one BF16 output rounding. The mode requires the Humming or Marlin SP path and an affirmative startup vote from every rank. It activates on dense BF16 tensors of at least 64 MiB on Hopper. It supports ragged owners, strided producer tensors and zero padding. The additional launch flag is `--muse-owner-partial-exchange`. [Official export](patch.diff) · [Launch contract and rule checks](validation.json)

At 16,384 tokens and TP8, the 8,192-channel attention return sends 224 MiB per rank and the 4,096-channel expert return sends 112 MiB. A conventional ring all-reduce of those two tensors would send 448 and 224 MiB respectively. The implementation takes seven and four exchange rounds, with receive arrays of 32 MiB plus 48 KiB and 28 MiB respectively. CUDA allocator pressure also includes the live producer, compact output, and a contiguous copy if required. The incumbent can use NVLS, whose actual cost on the judge is unknown. Expressing the hypothetical ring bytes saved on full chunks at peak link bandwidth gives 2.21% and 2.38% of the archived V41 16- and 32-user wall times. These ratios describe bytes at an assumed bandwidth and cannot predict a score. [Geometry](independent_geometry.json) · [Trace and numerical comparisons](independent_review.md)

Real Gloo processes replayed both model exits and the stock fallback. In 408 rank-cases spanning worlds of two, three, four and eight, 88,852 returned or padded BF16 components matched the ordered CPU reference. Independent simulated NCCL C calls routed 936,632 BF16 words through the pinned PyNccl wrappers. The production reducer compiled in 61 Hopper configurations. A separate interpreter replay verified all 328,164 FP32 accumulator words and the compiler's round-to-nearest BF16 lowering. The CPU interpreter's BF16 store truncates on precision-sensitive values, and native NVLS may use a different FP32 sum tree. Eight-H200 output agreement, graph memory, TTFT and elapsed time are open.
