# V191 fuses the gate/up projection and activation during target verification

V191 removes one CUDA launch and one BF16 gate/up write-and-read per eligible routed expert layer on V190. At 32 users and eight verification rows per user, uniform routing gives 256 live rows per rank. The projection and activation then avoid 8 MiB of logical traffic per layer, or 496 MiB across 62 layers. The two fixed projection arenas shrink from 48 to 32 MiB at 2,048 static route slots. At one user, the same estimates are 256 KiB per layer and 1.5 to 1 MiB of workspace. Hardware cache behavior and CUDA graph memory sharing determine actual device traffic and peak allocation. The Humming 0.1.12 decoder actually selects an 8-, 16- or 24-row W13 tile. Its two K-warps cooperatively reduce a 128-wide K tile. Two four-dimensional TMA descriptors gather matching 128-channel gate and up bands from the existing expert bank and the existing block-scale bank. Humming runs the native BF16 tensor operations and two-way K reduction, then writes its rounded BF16 fragments into shared memory. The epilogue consumes those fragments, decodes the local expert from the existing sorted route key, applies the expert scale and its BF16 rounding, and writes the BF16 SiLU product. The next projection reuses the gathered-input arena after the stream dependency. [Humming tuning](config.json) · [Independent epilogue and tensor-map replay](independent_epilogue.json)

V191 groups the first two K16 operations inside each K64 warp iteration. The compiled four-stage loop retains 32 HGMMA instructions and uses 12 full drains instead of 16. At K=4,096, each warpgroup executes 96 waits instead of 128. NVCC 12.9.86 compiled the original, fusion-only and combined variants to SM90a at 96 registers per thread and zero spills in all nine images. The three W2 cubins are byte-identical to V190’s inherited V189 images. The startup adapter validates the presence of both the matrix sequence and the four-dimensional tensor loads before accepting a kernel. Independent CPU replay checked 2,125,824 BF16 projection/activation outputs, every BF16 encoding in each operand, 10,616,832 descriptor addresses and 33,336,192 guard words. A delayed-completion test of the exact paired C++ implementation performed 1,062,912 pending source-register checks. The production planner, gather, optional W13/W2 dispatch and sparse finalizer produced matching outputs in 288 end-to-end cases, including moved packed banks, missing widths and failed descriptor creation. Ordinary grouped execution remains available separately at each unsupported width. **Serving speed is unmeasured.** The archived single-H200 Marlin expert call took 130.34 µs at eight input rows and ten local routes, 616.16 µs at 128/128 and 791.90 µs at 256/252. Those match decode sizes but time a different backend's whole expert call. At 4.8 TB/s peak, 8 MiB corresponds to 1.75 µs if every byte goes to HBM. The launch saved and the additional work issued during outstanding WGMMA are separate opportunities. The closest official serving result is V68’s 16-user private pass at 17,512.9 tokens/s/GPU. V191 has only local static and numerical validation and has not consumed a check.
