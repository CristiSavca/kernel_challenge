# V41 and V44 on matched H200 prefill and decode intervals

The V44 archive stopped before it saved a four-user client result. Its server log nevertheless covers both initial four-user cold waves. Across 19 interior 16,384-row intervals whose three consecutive scheduler reports agree on geometry, V41 averaged 1.4024 s and V44 averaged 1.2249 s. The difference was 177.5 ms per chunk. In the nine isolated single-user intervals with the same prompt length, prefix position and graph state, the difference was 139.3 ms. ## Separate expert timing

A physical single-H200 control with 16,224 local routes measured 6.61725 ms for compact Marlin and 5.83104 ms for the dense implementation with a 384-route admission threshold. Both that threshold and V44's admit the 16K case. If that exact saving were exposed on the critical rank at every one of 62 layers, the chunk saving would be 48.75 ms. That is 35.0% of the observed single-user difference and 27.5% at four users. Under that assumption the gaps remaining are 90.6 and 128.8 ms per chunk. They can include machine, routing skew, graph and host overhead, overlapping work and measurement alignment. The control used generated weights and different dispatch code. Its dense output differed from stock in 1.374% of BF16 words. Neither the recorded timings nor that output comparison establish the production kernel’s contribution to the V44 result. ## Captured verification

The public scheduler accumulates the same generated-token count for its throughput and speculative acceptance. Dividing mean accepted tokens per verification by generated tokens per second therefore estimates seconds per verification. Its 40-step report interval lets us exclude turns crossing a prefill boundary. Each run contains 25 complete single-user intervals. Nineteen cover the same repetition, turn and within-turn ordinal in both logs. Nine also match the full request prompt, cached prefix, requested and returned token counts. In those nine 40-step pairs the pooled V41/V44 step-time ratio is **1.00028×**, with median step times 15.841 and 15.895 ms. The rounded logged rates and up to 114 tokens of difference at the ending context position limit precision. This is a same-geometry control for captured verification. The request timings and their acceptance remain workload-dependent. [Window observations](check.json) · [Previous client-only control](../v265_native_serving_profile/README.md)

## What the cold improvement would have to deliver

To cut the old four-user 16K interval to one third or one quarter, another 0.757 or 0.874 seconds would have to come off the V44 mean. For the full scoring ladder, V271's separate scenario shortens all phases enough to reach a coupled score target. Its 32-user cold wave would need an average saving of 10.18 or 12.91 ms per equivalent sparse layer. The 177.5 ms four-user chunk difference translates to 2.86 ms per layer if attributed uniformly. Transferring it unchanged to the 32-user wave would leave the required 3× and 4× cold savings 3.56 and 4.51 times larger. There is no V44 32-user control or V68 kernel breakdown in these recordings.
